# Unit 2: Project Proposal and Planning

## Problem statement

Employees do not consistently recognize common phishing indicators or know the correct
action to take when they receive a suspicious email, link, attachment, or request for
sensitive information. Most awareness programs do not measure whether training actually
improves these skills.

## Research questions

- **RQ1.** To what extent does the training improve the ability of participants to
  correctly identify phishing and legitimate messages?
- **RQ2.** To what extent does the training improve the ability of participants to choose
  the correct response, such as reporting or verifying?
- **RQ3.** Which phishing indicators show the greatest improvement, and which ones remain
  difficult after the training?

## SMART goals

| Goal | Target | Due |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Training package covering all five indicators plus verification and reporting | Week 4 |
| 2 | Matched 20-question pre- and post-assessments at equal difficulty | Week 4 |
| 3 | Average assessment score up at least 20 percentage points | Week 7 |
| 4 | At least 80% of participants scoring 80% or higher post-training | Week 7 |

## Participants

10 to 20 volunteer adults, at least 18 years old, who use email at least weekly and have
basic computer skills. People in cybersecurity roles or who helped build the training are
excluded because they would skew the results.

## Evaluation method

Participants complete the pre-training assessment first to set a baseline. Each assessment
has 10 knowledge questions and 10 scenario questions. In each scenario the participant
reviews a safe mock email, decides whether it is phishing or legitimate, and chooses an
action. Participants then complete the training and take the post-training assessment
within one week.

Six metrics are compared: average score, percentage-point improvement, percentage of
participants reaching 80%, correct response rate on scenario questions, accuracy for each
of the five indicators, and the false alarm rate on legitimate emails.

## Success criteria

The project is successful if all deliverables are complete, Goals 3 and 4 are met, and the
correct response rate on scenario questions improves by at least 20 percentage points.

## Ethics and security

- Informed consent before any assessment.
- Results tracked by participant ID, never by name.
- Only the minimum data needed is collected.
- Mock content uses fictional names and disabled links, with no live malware or credential
  forms.
- Results stored encrypted with least-privilege access (Joint Task Force, 2020).
- The project follows the ACM Code of Ethics (Association for Computing Machinery, 2018).
- All participant data deleted after the final report is submitted.

## Feasibility

Technically, operationally, and economically feasible. Estimated cost is close to zero.
The main risk is schedule, managed by recruiting participants from Week 2 and tracking
progress weekly.
