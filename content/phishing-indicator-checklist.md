# Phishing Indicator Checklist

Print this and keep it next to your monitor. Check the message against all five
points before you click anything.

## 1. Suspicious sender address
Look at the full address, not the display name. Check for a public mail domain on a
business message, a misspelled company name, or extra words added to a real domain.

## 2. Misleading or mismatched link
Hover over the link and read the address that appears. If the visible text and the
real destination do not match, treat the message as suspicious.

## 3. Urgent or threatening language
Watch for deadlines, account closures, and warnings about consequences. Urgency is
used to stop you from checking the message carefully.

## 4. Request for credentials or sensitive information
No legitimate internal process asks for your password by email. Treat any request
for a password, a code, a card number, or gift cards as phishing.

## 5. Unexpected attachment
An attachment you did not ask for is a risk, even when the sender looks familiar.
Invoices, shipping notices, and resumes are the most commonly used covers.

## If a message fails any check
Do not click, do not reply, and do not open the attachment. Verify through a channel
you already trust, then report it.
